This tutorial shows you how to change a color in a Canva photo while retaining the shading and texture of the original image.
Select the photo and click Edit, then open Adjust.
For a targeted change, use the available area selection controls to select the subject or paint over the object you want to edit.
Scroll to the color section and select the color swatch that represents the jacket.
Move Hue to shift that color toward your preferred shade.
Adjust saturation to control its intensity, then use brightness to keep the folds and shadows looking natural.
Make small changes and compare them with the original rather than immediately pushing every slider to its maximum.
Zoom in around the object's edges.
If other objects use a similar color, check whether they change too.
Refine the selected area if your editor provides click or brush selection.
If the object is white, gray or very dark, hue adjustments may have little effect because there is not much existing color to shift.
For a larger transformation, try Magic Edit if your plan includes it.
Select the object and describe the desired change, such as changing the jacket to blue.
Here's the part many tutorials skip.
A photo contains many related shades rather than one flat color.
A hue adjustment is useful for a natural recolor, but it does not guarantee an exact brand color match across every highlight and shadow.
For product images where exact color matters, compare the result carefully with a reference.
Keep the version that preserves believable detail, then download the edited photo as PNG or JPG.
