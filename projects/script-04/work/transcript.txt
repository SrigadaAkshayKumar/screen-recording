This tutorial shows you how to remove unwanted white edges from a Canva export,
with separate checks for digital images and printed designs.
Open the design and zoom in around all four edges.
Select the background image or coloured shape and check that it reaches the complete page boundary.
If an image is meant to cover the whole page, right-click it and choose Set Image as Background.
Otherwise enlarge it slightly so its edges extend beyond the canvas.
Check that a white rectangle or border has not been placed above it.
Download a test image and inspect the saved file at its actual size.
A line visible only while zooming in the editor may be a preview artefact,
so judge the exported file before making more changes.
For a printed design, open File, then Rulers and Guides and enable Show Print Bleed.
Extend the background into the bleed area instead of stopping at the trim line.
Choose PDF Print when downloading.
Enable crop marks and bleed if your print provider requests them,
and confirm that the provider will trim the sheet to the intended finished size.
Here's the part many tutorials skip.
Crop marks create extra space around the artwork.
That space is expected in a print-ready PDF
and should not be confused with a white border inside the finished design.
If your home printer leaves an even white border,
check its borderless printing support and paper settings.
Changing the Canva background cannot make a printer print into an area its hardware cannot reach.
Export one final test and confirm whether the edge problem belongs to the artwork,
the PDF setup, or the printer.
