SCRIPT 1

HOW TO MAKE AN ANIMATED FILMSTRIP IN CANVA

This tutorial shows you how to build a moving filmstrip from your own photos in Canva.

Create a landscape Video design. Open Elements, add a black rectangle, and stretch it into a wide horizontal strip across the middle of the page. Leave the page background a different color so the strip stands out.

Open Elements again, go to Frames, and add a square or rectangular photo frame. Resize it so there is black space above and below it, then duplicate it to create three evenly spaced windows.

Drag a different uploaded photo into each frame. Double-click each photo to adjust its position without moving the frame itself.

For the film perforations, add a small white square above the first photo. Duplicate it across the top edge, then copy the row along the bottom. Keep these squares inside the black strip.

Select the rectangle, photo frames, and white squares, then click Group. Leave the page background outside the group.

Move the group toward the right side. With it selected, open Animate and choose Create an animation. Drag the entire filmstrip horizontally toward the left, then release. Adjust the movement settings and preview the result.

Here’s the useful detail: everything must move as one group. Animating the photos separately can make them slide away from the black strip.

Adjust the scene length to give the movement enough time, and keep any heading outside the animated group.

Finally, choose Share, Download, and MP4 Video. Play the downloaded file to check the movement.

SCRIPT 2

HOW TO ADD 3D SHAPES IN CANVA

This tutorial shows you how to add actual rotatable 3D elements to a Canva design.

Open a presentation or social media design, then select Elements. Find the 3D category, or search for 3D and browse the available objects.

Choose a simple object, such as a sphere or cube, and add it to the page. Select the object and drag to change its viewing angle. Then resize it and position it beside your text.

If the toolbar displays color swatches, click one and choose a color that matches your design. Some objects have several editable colors, while others keep their original materials.

For a clean example, place a blue sphere beside a bold heading on a light background. Keep enough space between the object and the lettering so the shape supports the message.

You can also generate a custom object. Open Magic Media, select the 3D tab, describe the object, and choose Generate 3D. Try a short prompt such as a smooth orange cube with rounded corners.

Here’s the important limitation: a normal 3D-looking graphic is different from a rotatable 3D element. If an item only rotates flat on the page, return to the 3D category.

Canva currently exports these 3D objects as static images. Interactive rotation is available in supported presentations and published websites, rather than in a downloaded image or animated 3D export.

Preview your design in its intended format before sharing, and use a clear starting angle that looks good even when the object is still.

SCRIPT 3

HOW TO ADD BLEED LINES TO BUSINESS CARDS IN CANVA

This tutorial shows you how to prepare the bleed area on a Canva business card before printing.

Open your business card design and confirm that its finished dimensions match the size requested by your printer. The finished size is the card after trimming.

Open File, then Rulers and guides, and enable Show print bleed. Turn on Show margins as well.

Now check every edge of the card. Extend background colors, photos, and decorative shapes through the bleed area. A background that stops at the finished edge can leave a thin white line when the cutting position varies slightly.

Keep your name, phone number, logo, and other important content comfortably inside the margins. These items should not extend into the area that will be cut away.

For example, if a blue rectangle forms the card background, enlarge that rectangle. Do not enlarge the entire design, because that also pushes the text closer to the edge.

When both sides are ready, select Share and Download. Choose PDF, use the Print preset, and open Advanced Settings. Enable Crop marks and bleed if your printer requests them, then download the file.

Here’s the important distinction: bleed guides help you design, while crop marks in the exported PDF show where the card should be trimmed. You do not need to draw either one manually.

Open the PDF and inspect both sides at a readable zoom. Check the backgrounds, spelling, and text spacing, then confirm the printer accepts Canva’s fixed bleed settings before sending the file.

SCRIPT 4

HOW TO DOWNLOAD A CANVA INVOICE AS PDF

This tutorial shows you how to download the invoice for a Canva subscription or purchase as a PDF.

Start on the Canva homepage and check that you are signed into the account used for the payment. If you work in several teams, switch to the team where the purchase was made.

Open your profile menu and choose Settings. Then select Orders and invoices.

Find the transaction you need. Use the date or purchase-type filters to narrow the list, or search for an invoice number when you have one.

Check the payment date and amount before downloading. This helps you avoid saving the invoice for the wrong renewal or print order.

Select Download invoice beside the transaction. In some layouts, open the three-dot menu first. Another layout labels the action View invoice, which also downloads the PDF.

Open the downloaded file and check the invoice number, billing details, and total. Give the file a useful name containing the purchase date so you can find it later.

Here’s the main troubleshooting point: purchases billed through Apple or Google may need their invoice or receipt from that provider. A payment made through an app store will not necessarily appear like a direct Canva website purchase.

If the transaction is missing, check the Canva confirmation email and verify the account and team again. Team permissions can also affect which invoices you can see.

You can now store the PDF with your purchase records or attach it wherever the invoice is needed.

SCRIPT 5

HOW TO PRINT A LARGE IMAGE ON MULTIPLE PAGES IN CANVA

This tutorial shows you how to design a large image in Canva and print it across several sheets using Acrobat Reader.

In Canva, create a design at the final poster dimensions. For example, choose a custom size of sixty by forty centimeters for a landscape poster.

Upload the image and fit it to the page. Check the crop carefully so the main subject stays inside the finished poster. Use a sufficiently detailed original image because enlarging a small picture can make the print blurry.

Choose Share, Download, and PDF with the Print preset. Download the single poster page without adding crop marks for this example.

Open the PDF in the desktop version of Adobe Acrobat Reader. Open Print, choose your printer, and set the paper size to the sheets you actually have, such as A4.

Under Page Sizing and Handling, select Poster. This divides the large PDF page across several sheets.

Set Tile Scale to one hundred percent to preserve the poster size created in Canva. Add a small overlap, such as five millimeters, and enable cut marks if helpful.

Check the preview and sheet count before printing. A small change in overlap or paper orientation can change how many sheets are required.

Here’s the important part: this method uses Canva for the artwork and Acrobat for the tiled printing. Choosing Multiple in Acrobat does the opposite by fitting several document pages onto one sheet.

Print the tiles, trim the overlapping edges as needed, align the picture, and tape the sheets together from behind.

SCRIPT 6

HOW TO CREATE A WATERMARK IN CANVA

This tutorial shows you how to create a reusable text or logo watermark in Canva.

Open the photo or design you want to watermark. Press T to add a text box, then type your name, website, or brand name.

Choose a clear font and a color that remains visible against the picture. White often works on darker areas, while a dark gray can work on light backgrounds.

If you already have a logo, open Uploads and add that instead. A logo with a transparent background is easier to place because it does not bring a solid rectangle into the photo.

Position the watermark where it will be visible without covering the most important detail. Select it, open Transparency, and reduce the value until the image underneath remains clear. Start around thirty percent and adjust for the actual picture.

For a diagonal watermark, use the rotation handle and place the text across the center. For a simple signature, keep it smaller near a corner.

To reuse the watermark, copy the text or logo into another Canva design. You can also keep a separate design containing your watermark versions in light and dark colors.

If you need a reusable image file, place only the watermark on a separate page, choose Share and Download, select PNG, and enable Transparent background. That export option requires an eligible paid plan.

Here’s the useful distinction: element transparency controls how faint the watermark looks. Transparent background removes the page behind it.

Download the finished watermarked photo as PNG or JPG and inspect its readability.

SCRIPT 7

HOW TO MAKE AI VOICEOVERS SOUND REAL IN CANVA

This tutorial shows you how to make a Canva AI voiceover sound more natural by improving the script and voice choice.

Open a video or presentation, select Apps, and search for AI Voice. Open Canva’s AI Voice tool and paste a short section of your narration.

Choose the correct language and a voice style that fits the content. For a tutorial, start with a clear, conversational voice rather than a dramatic delivery.

Before generating, read the text aloud. Replace long sentences with shorter ones and remove phrases you would not naturally say in conversation.

Use full stops and commas where a speaker should pause. Write unusual abbreviations and numbers in the form you want spoken. For example, write one point five if a numeric expression keeps being pronounced incorrectly.

Generate the short sample and listen with headphones. Pay attention to names, emphasis, and the space between instructions.

If a line sounds awkward, rewrite that line and generate another sample. Changing the wording often produces a better result than repeatedly generating the exact same sentence.

Once the sample sounds right, generate the remaining sections using the same voice. Add the narration to the timeline and arrange the sections in order.

Here’s the part people often miss: a voiceover needs room to breathe between screen actions. Move or trim the audio so each instruction lands when the viewer can follow it.

Keep background music below the narration and preview the complete video. Listen for abrupt joins, repeated words, and sudden volume changes before exporting.

SCRIPT 8

HOW TO MAKE A FOLDABLE BUSINESS CARD IN CANVA

This tutorial shows you how to design a business card that folds in half using a custom Canva layout.

First, decide the finished folded size and folding direction. For this example, the closed card will be eighty-five millimeters wide and fifty-five millimeters tall, with a horizontal fold.

Create a custom design measuring eighty-five by one hundred and ten millimeters. This is the unfolded sheet, before adding any printer-required bleed.

Open File, then Rulers and guides, and show the rulers. Add a horizontal guide halfway down the page, at fifty-five millimeters. The guide marks the fold.

Use page one for the outside spread. In a top-fold layout, place the front cover on the lower half. The upper half becomes the back cover and usually needs its artwork rotated one hundred and eighty degrees relative to the front.

Add page two for the inside spread. Place the inside information in the two panels and keep text away from the fold and outer edges.

Here’s the essential check: make a paper mockup before ordering. Mark each panel, print both spreads, fold the sheet, and confirm which way the covers and inside text face.

Duplex settings and printer templates can change the required orientation, so use your print provider’s panel layout for the final file.

Remove any actual line you drew to represent the fold. A ruler guide will not print, but a decorative line will.

Download a PDF with the Print preset and the bleed settings requested by your printer. Proof a folded sample before producing the full batch.

SCRIPT 9

HOW TO REMOVE BACKGROUNDS IN BULK IN CANVA

This tutorial shows you how to remove several photo backgrounds at once using Canva’s bulk background remover.

Start from the Canva homepage and open Projects. Create a folder for the photos you want to process, then upload your images into that folder.

Use a small test batch first. Product photos with clear edges and a background that contrasts with the subject are a useful starting point.

Return to the folder listing, hover over the folder, and open its three-dot menu. Select Remove background.

Canva processes the supported uploaded photos and creates a separate folder containing the edited versions. The new folder name includes Edited, while the original folder stays available.

Open the edited folder and check each cutout. Look closely around hair, transparent objects, thin straps, and any area that blends into the original background.

If you only want certain photos, open the original folder, tick their selection boxes, and choose BG Remover from the toolbar at the bottom.

This selected-photo method adds the edited copies to the same folder, so check which versions you are opening before placing them into a design.

Here’s the important limit: bulk background removal handles up to fifty uploaded images at a time and requires an eligible paid plan. Canva library photos or complete designs inside the folder are not processed like uploaded photos.

If the folder option is unavailable, reduce the folder to fifty images or fewer and check your plan access.

Use the finished cutouts in a new design or download the processed images as transparent PNGs.

SCRIPT 10

HOW TO CROP AN IMAGE DIAGONALLY IN CANVA

This tutorial shows you how to create a diagonal image crop in Canva using a shaped frame.

Open your design and go to Elements. Open the Frames category, then search for triangle frame or diagonal frame.

Choose a frame with a visible diagonal edge. Make sure you are adding an image frame rather than a decorative triangle graphic. A frame acts as a container for your photo.

Resize the frame on the page. For a simple split layout, place a large right-triangle frame against one side, leaving the opposite side available for a heading or another image.

Open Uploads and drag your photo directly over the frame. Wait until the photo fills the frame before releasing.

Double-click the photo to enter its crop controls. Move the image inside the shape and use the corner handles to zoom until the subject is positioned correctly.

Click outside to finish. Rotate the frame if you need the diagonal to slope in the opposite direction, then check the subject again.

For example, place a portrait inside the triangle and keep the person’s face away from the diagonal edge. This makes the crop look deliberate instead of accidentally cutting through an important detail.

Here’s the useful detail: rotating an ordinary rectangular photo does not create the same diagonal mask. The frame defines which part of the image remains visible.

The hidden image content stays available, so you can double-click and reposition it later.

Download the complete design as PNG or JPG, or use a transparent-background PNG when your plan supports it.

SCRIPT 11

HOW TO BULK CREATE DESIGNS IN CANVA USING CSV AND GOOGLE SHEETS

This tutorial shows you how to turn a Google Sheets table into multiple Canva designs using a CSV file.

In Google Sheets, create clearly named columns such as Name, Offer, and Website. Put one complete design’s information on each row.

For this example, add three rows with different business names and offers. Keep the headings in the first row and remove empty rows inside the table.

Choose File, Download, and Comma-separated values to save the current sheet as a CSV file.

Open your Canva template and add a separate text box for each changing field. Leave logos and other shared decorations unconnected if they should stay identical.

Open Apps and select Bulk Create. Choose Upload data, select your CSV, and continue.

Use Match and fill fields if offered, then inspect the matches. Select a text box, choose Match data, and connect it to the correct column. Repeat for the remaining fields.

For example, the business-name text box should use Name, while the promotional sentence should use Offer.

Preview the results and check the longest entries for awkward wrapping. Adjust the template before generating the batch.

Open the creation options and choose one design with multiple pages if you want all versions together. Then create the designs and open the result.

Here’s the important limitation: a downloaded CSV is a snapshot. Editing the Google Sheet later does not automatically refresh this generated batch.

Download a new CSV and regenerate when your data changes. Bulk Create also requires a supported Canva plan on desktop.

SCRIPT 12

HOW TO EXPORT A CANVA LOGO AS A WEBSITE FAVICON

This tutorial shows you how to export a Canva logo as a small website favicon.

Start with a square Canva design. Use a square measuring five hundred and twelve pixels on each side as your working size.

Copy your logo into the square. If it includes a long business name, use the symbol or a simple initial instead. A favicon needs to remain recognizable when displayed very small in a browser tab.

Enlarge the symbol so it fills most of the square, but leave a little clear space around the edges. Center it on the page.

For example, use a bold letter inside a colored circle rather than shrinking a full business-card layout into the icon.

Check the design at a small zoom. If thin lines disappear or letters become unreadable, simplify the artwork before exporting.

Select Share and Download, then choose PNG. Enable Transparent background if your plan supports it and you want an icon without a solid square behind it. Otherwise, choose an intentional background color.

Download the file and upload it through your website platform’s favicon or site-icon setting.

For a Canva website, open the dropdown beside Publish and choose Settings. In the Site tab, select the favicon, upload the square image, choose Done, and publish the update.

Here’s the useful distinction: PNG works for Canva’s favicon upload, while another website platform may request different dimensions or an ICO file. Follow that platform’s requirements.

Finally, open the published website and check the browser tab. A cached icon may need a refresh before the new version appears.

SCRIPT 13

HOW TO CHANGE YOUR VOICE IN CANVA

This tutorial shows you how to change a recorded voice using a voice-changing app inside Canva.

Prepare a short recording of your own voice and save a copy of the original. A recording with little background noise gives the app a better starting point.

Open a video design in Canva, select Apps, and search for AI Voice Changer. Open the app listing and review its available voices, account requirements, and usage allowance.

Upload your audio file into the app. The voice changer described by Canva supports formats including MP3, WAV, M4A, and MP4, although you should check the specific app you open.

Choose a replacement voice and generate a short test. Listen for understandable words, natural pauses, and any distortion on louder sounds.

If the result is too extreme, choose a less dramatic voice or adjust any controls the app provides. Keep the original recording available so you can compare both versions.

Once you are satisfied, add the changed voice to your design. Align the audio with the correct scene, trim unwanted silence, and adjust the volume.

If the original video already contains your speech, mute or reduce that original audio so both voices do not play together.

Here’s the important distinction: changing a recording is different from choosing a text-to-speech voice. A voice changer processes audio you supply; AI Voice generates speech from written text.

Preview the complete scene and check that the replacement remains synchronized with the visuals.

Download the finished video as MP4, then play the exported file to confirm that only the intended voice is audible.

SCRIPT 14

HOW TO FREEZE A FRAME IN CANVA VIDEO

This tutorial shows you how to create a freeze-frame moment in a Canva video by inserting a still image between two moving sections.

First, open your source video in VLC Media Player and pause on the moment you want to hold. Open the Video menu and choose Take Snapshot to save that frame as an image.

Using a video snapshot avoids capturing the player buttons or surrounding desktop.

Return to Canva and add the same video to a Video design. Move the playhead to the matching moment, select the clip, and choose More, then Split.

You now have a section before the freeze and a section that resumes afterward.

Upload the saved snapshot to Canva and insert it on the main timeline between those two sections. Set the image duration to around two seconds for this example.

Resize and crop the image so it matches the video exactly. If the footage fills the page, the still should fill the page in the same way.

Preview the join at normal speed. If the picture jumps, adjust the split point or capture a closer matching frame.

Keep transitions off at both edges of the still for a clean, sudden freeze. You can add a label or arrow during the hold if you want to explain something.

Here’s the part to check separately: inserting a still image does not automatically create the audio treatment you want. Decide whether speech, music, or silence should continue during the hold, then arrange the audio tracks.

Export as MP4 and check that movement pauses and resumes smoothly.

SCRIPT 15

HOW TO CHANGE VIDEO TIMELINE DURATION IN CANVA

This tutorial shows you how to change the length of scenes and clips in Canva’s video timeline.

Open your Video design and make sure the timeline is visible. If you are in Page Mode, use the page thumbnail’s pencil control to switch into the Video Timeline.

Select the clip or scene you want to adjust. Zoom into the timeline so its start and end are easy to see.

To shorten a video clip, drag its right edge toward the left. To restore more of the original footage, drag the edge outward, as long as unused source footage is available.

Dragging the left edge changes where that section begins. Preview the result so you do not remove the start of an important action or sentence.

For a scene that needs an exact duration, select the scene and use the clock control in the floating toolbar. Enter the duration in seconds.

You can also select several scenes and use that timing control to give them the same duration.

If a title disappears too early, select its own timeline strip and extend the appropriate edge. The title has separate timing from the scene beneath it.

Here’s the key distinction: timeline zoom only changes how the timeline looks on screen. It does not make the finished video longer or shorter.

Likewise, moving a clip changes its position, while trimming its edges changes the portion that plays.

Check the final scene, narration, and music after adjusting durations. Then preview from the previous cut to confirm that the new timing feels right before downloading the video.

SCRIPT 16

HOW TO SET UP A BRAND KIT IN CANVA

This tutorial shows you how to organize your logo, colors, and fonts in a Canva Brand Kit.

From the Canva homepage, open Brand. Open the Brand Kit dropdown, choose Create new Brand Kit, enter your brand name, and select Create.

Start with Logos. Choose Add to category, select Upload brand assets, and upload the logo files you use regularly.

Include a main logo and, if you have them, light and dark versions. Clear filenames make it easier to choose the right one later.

Next, open Colors. Add a palette and enter your brand’s color values. Use exact hex codes rather than trying to match each color by eye.

For a simple example, save your main blue, a dark text color, and a light background color. Name the palette so it is easy to recognize.

Open Fonts and set the heading and body styles. Choose the typefaces, weights, and sizes you want to use, then save the settings.

If your plan supports custom font uploads, add the licensed font files you need before setting those styles.

Now open a design and select Brand from the editor’s side panel. Choose your kit, add a logo, and use its colors and text styles in the layout.

Here’s the important limitation: available Brand Kit features depend on your plan and role. In a team, you may need an administrator or brand designer to upload assets or change settings.

Setting up the kit also does not automatically restyle every older design. Open a design and apply the saved assets where needed.

SCRIPT 17

HOW TO CREATE STICKERS IN CANVA

This tutorial shows you how to turn a photo into a sticker-style graphic in Canva.

Create a square design and upload a photo with a clear subject. An object or portrait with visible edges is easier to turn into a clean sticker.

Select the photo and use BG Remover to remove its background. Check the edges before continuing, especially around hair or small details. Background removal requires eligible Canva access.

With the cutout selected, open Edit and find Shadows. Choose Glow to build an outline around the subject.

Set the glow color to white, reduce Blur to zero, and increase its strength so the border looks solid. Adjust the size until the white edge is visible without swallowing small details.

Place a temporary colored shape behind the sticker to inspect the border. This reveals gaps or leftover background patches.

You can add a short caption underneath using a bold font. Keep the wording brief so it stays readable when the sticker is small.

When you are happy, remove the temporary background shape. Select Share and Download, choose PNG, and enable Transparent background if your plan supports that export.

Here’s the important detail: the white outline should remain part of the sticker, while the empty space outside it should be transparent. A white page alone does not create the same result.

Download the PNG and test it over a colored background.

For a physical sticker, send the artwork to your printer using their required size and cut-line specifications. A visible white outline is not automatically a machine-readable cutting path.

HOW TO CREATE AN INVOICE IN CANVA

This tutorial shows you how to create a client invoice from a Canva template.

From the Canva homepage, search for Invoice and choose a template with enough room for your services or products.

Replace the sample business name, logo, address, and contact details with your own. Add the customer’s billing information in the client section.

Enter a unique invoice number, the issue date, and the payment due date. Keep the date format consistent throughout the document.

SCRIPT 20

HOW TO APPLY TWO TEXT EFFECTS AT ONCE IN CANVA

This tutorial shows you how to combine an outline and a shadow in Canva using two aligned text layers.

Open your design, add a text box, and type the heading. Choose the final font, size, spacing, and alignment before making a copy.

Duplicate the text using Control and D on Windows or Command and D on a Mac.

Select the front copy, open Effects, and choose Outline. Pick a contrasting outline color and adjust its thickness so the words remain easy to read.

Select the second copy using Position and Layers. Give that copy a shadow effect, labeled Drop or Shadow depending on your editor, then adjust its direction, distance, and softness.

Move the shadow copy behind the outlined copy using the layer controls.

Select both text boxes, open Position, and align their horizontal centers and vertical middles. The letters should overlap exactly, with only the shadow extending beyond the front text.

If a duplicate was offset when created, align it precisely instead of estimating its position.

Here’s the practical advantage: the two effects stay on separate copies, so choosing one effect cannot replace the other layer’s effect.

Group the copies when you are satisfied. Move and resize the group to keep the layers together.

To change the wording later, edit both copies and keep their fonts identical. Otherwise, the letters and shadow can stop lining up.

Preview the heading against its actual background and download a test image. If the shadow is hard to see, increase its offset slightly or choose a stronger contrasting color.
