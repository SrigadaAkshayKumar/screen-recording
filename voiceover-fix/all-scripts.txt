SCRIPT 1

HOW TO ADD A LOGO TO A VIDEO IN CANVA

This tutorial shows you how to place your logo over a video in Canva and keep it visible for the right amount of time.

(On screen: A video design open in Canva, with a transparent logo ready to upload.)

Open your video project and go to Uploads in the left sidebar. Click Upload files, select your logo, and wait for it to appear. A PNG with an existing transparent background works well because it avoids a white rectangle around the logo.

Click the uploaded logo to add it as a separate element. Drag a corner handle to resize it, then move it into a corner of the video. Leave a little space between the logo and the edge so it stays readable when you post the video.

If the logo disappears behind your footage, select Position, open Layers, and move the logo above the video.

Select the logo and look at its layer in the video timeline. Adjust the start and end edges so it appears throughout the section you want to brand. For a design with separate scenes or pages, check each one and copy the logo onto any scene where it is missing.

Here's the part many tutorials skip: placing the logo on the canvas does not guarantee it covers the entire finished video. Preview every scene change and the last few seconds to check its timing.

When everything looks right, choose Share, Download, and MP4 Video. Your downloaded video now includes the logo.

SCRIPT 2

HOW TO ADD A WATERMARK TO A VIDEO IN CANVA

This tutorial shows you how to add a subtle text or logo watermark to your video in Canva.

(On screen: A video playing in Canva with no watermark.)

Open your video design. To use a logo, go to Uploads and add your logo file. To use text, open Text, add a text box, and type your channel name, website, or business name.

Resize the watermark and place it where it remains readable without covering the main subject. A corner is a useful starting point, but check whether your captions or the platform's on-screen buttons will overlap it.

Select the watermark, click Transparency in the toolbar, and lower the value. Try around forty to sixty as a starting point, then preview it over both light and dark parts of your footage. A value of one hundred makes the element fully visible, while zero hides it.

Use Position and Layers if you need to bring the watermark above the video. Adjust its timeline layer so it covers the intended section, and add it to other scenes when necessary.

Here's the part many tutorials skip: a watermark that looks clear over one frame may disappear over another. Test a white version, a dark version, or a small contrasting backing until it stays visible during playback.

Finish by downloading the design as an MP4 Video. Check the saved file to confirm the watermark remains visible through the final frame.

SCRIPT 3

HOW TO BLUR A VIDEO IN CANVA

This tutorial shows you a reliable workaround for using genuinely blurred video footage in a Canva project.

(On screen: An unblurred clip in Canva, followed by the same source clip in CapCut's online editor.)

Canva's regular photo Blur tool does not process moving video. For this method, we will prepare a blurred MP4 in CapCut's online editor and then finish the design in Canva.

Upload your original clip to CapCut and add it to the timeline. Open Effects, choose Video effects, and browse the blur category. Apply a basic blur effect, then adjust its strength until the footage has the amount of softness you want.

Extend the effect strip from the beginning to the end of the clip. Play the footage to check that the effect covers the whole video, including the final frames.

Export the result as an MP4. Keep the original dimensions when possible so you can still crop and resize it in Canva.

Return to Canva, open Uploads, and upload the blurred MP4. Add it to your video design and adjust its size, position, and duration. You can now place sharp text, graphics, or a logo above the blurred footage.

Here's the part many tutorials skip: lowering a video's transparency changes how see-through it is; it does not blur the moving picture. Use a clip with the blur actually rendered into it.

Preview your Canva design and download the finished result as an MP4 Video.

SCRIPT 4

HOW TO BLUR A FACE IN A CANVA VIDEO

This tutorial shows you how to blur moving faces in footage used in Canva with the BGBlur Face Blur app.

(On screen: A Canva video project and the Apps search panel.)

Open your design, select Apps in the left sidebar, and search for BGBlur or Face Blur. Open the Face Blur integration from BGBlur. This is a third-party tool, so check the credit requirements and file limits shown in its panel before processing.

Connect your BGBlur account if prompted. Upload the original video through the app's upload control, then choose the blur strength. If the app offers different styles, select the one you want to use.

Run the face-blurring action and wait for the processed result. Add that version to your Canva design, replacing the unblurred footage in the section you are editing.

Play the entire clip and check that every face you intend to hide remains covered. Pay special attention when someone turns sideways, moves quickly, or enters the frame. Increase the blur strength or process the clip again if details remain recognizable.

Here's the part many tutorials skip: a blur-shaped graphic placed over one frame does not automatically follow a moving face. You need a processed video with tracking, or you must adjust the cover throughout the movement.

If the integration is unavailable or your clip exceeds its limits, process it on the BGBlur website and upload the resulting video to Canva.

Finish your text and layout, then export the completed design as an MP4 Video.

SCRIPT 5

HOW TO BLUR THE BACKGROUND OF A VIDEO THAT DOESN'T FIT THE CANVAS IN CANVA

This tutorial shows you how to fill empty space around a video with a moving blurred backdrop in Canva.

(On screen: A vertical Canva design containing a landscape clip with empty space above and below.)

Prepare two versions of the same footage: the sharp original and a blurred copy. To create the blurred copy, open the source clip in CapCut's online editor, apply a blur video effect across the entire clip, and export an MP4 without changing its duration.

Upload both files to Canva. Add the blurred version first and enlarge it until it covers the whole canvas. Some of this background copy will be cropped, which is fine because its purpose is to fill the empty areas.

Add the sharp original as a separate video element. Keep its proportions and resize it until the full picture fits inside the canvas. Use Position to center it and keep its layer above the blurred version.

Match the timeline start points and the source sections used by both clips. Keep their speed and duration the same so the backdrop follows the main footage. Set the background copy's volume to zero to prevent doubled sound.

Here's the part many tutorials skip: this effect blurs an enlarged copy behind the video. It does not remove or blur the real background around a person inside the sharp foreground clip.

Play the beginning, middle, and end to check alignment. Once the empty space is filled and the sound plays once, download your design as an MP4 Video.

SCRIPT 6

HOW TO REMOVE A VIDEO BACKGROUND IN CANVA

This tutorial shows you how to remove the background from a video and place the subject over a new design in Canva.

(On screen: A short uploaded video with a clearly visible person.)

Open a video design and upload your footage through Uploads. Add the clip to the canvas, wait for it to finish processing, and select the video itself.

Click BG Remover in the editor toolbar. If your layout places this control inside the editing panel, open Edit and look for the background-removal tool. Video background removal is included with eligible paid Canva plans.

Wait while Canva processes the clip. Then play it and inspect the edges around the subject, especially hair, hands, and fast movements.

Choose a new background by adding a color, image, or video behind the cutout. Open Position and Layers if you need to move that background below the subject. Resize and position the foreground clip to suit your layout.

Here's the part many tutorials skip: Canva currently limits this feature to videos whose original length is under ten minutes. Trimming a long source inside the design does not change the length of the uploaded file. Export a shorter source clip first when necessary.

Use footage with a clear subject and good lighting for cleaner edges. Preview the whole processed section before downloading.

Export as an MP4 Video to save the subject combined with your new background. This gives you a finished video with the background you designed.

SCRIPT 7

HOW TO MAKE A VERTICAL VIDEO IN CANVA

This tutorial shows you how to create a vertical video in Canva with a nine-by-sixteen layout.

(On screen: Canva's homepage and a blank portrait video design.)

Click Create a design and search for Mobile Video. Open a blank portrait video design. A canvas measuring one thousand eighty pixels wide by nineteen hundred twenty pixels tall is a practical full-HD size for this format.

Go to Uploads, upload your video clips, and add the first one to the design. Arrange additional clips in the order you want them to play.

For landscape footage, decide how to frame it. You can enlarge the clip to fill the vertical canvas and crop the sides, or keep the full picture visible with space around it. Double-click the video to adjust its crop and keep the important action in view.

Trim unnecessary footage and add text or graphics. Keep essential words and faces away from the outer edges, particularly the bottom and right side where social apps often display controls.

Here's the part many tutorials skip: changing the canvas to portrait does not improve the resolution of your original video. Enlarging a small clip can make it soft, so use a clear source and avoid zooming farther than necessary.

Play your design from start to finish. Check the crop again whenever the subject moves.

Choose Share, Download, and MP4 Video. Select a suitable quality option and save the finished vertical video.

HOW TO MAKE YOUTUBE SHORTS IN CANVA

This tutorial shows you how to turn your footage into a short vertical video for YouTube using Canva.

(On screen: A portrait video design with a short uploaded clip.)

Start a blank Mobile Video design, or search Canva's templates for YouTube Shorts and choose a portrait layout. Use a nine-by-sixteen canvas so the video fills a vertical phone screen.

Upload your footage and place it in the design. Trim the opening so viewers reach the useful action quickly. For this example, build a video of around thirty seconds with one clear point.

Add a short headline using Text. Place it where it is easy to read without covering the subject. Keep the font large enough for a phone screen and remove any template text that does not belong in your video.

If the clip contains speech, select it and open Captions from the toolbar, or find Captions through Text or Apps. Generate the captions, review the spelling, and choose a readable style.

Here's the part many tutorials skip: a caption can be correctly timed and still be hidden by YouTube's interface. Keep the important text away from the bottom edge and the column of buttons on the right.

HOW TO MAKE QUOTE REELS FOR INSTAGRAM IN CANVA

This tutorial shows you how to create an Instagram quote Reel with a moving background and readable text in Canva.

(On screen: A blank portrait design, a background clip, and a short quote.)

Search Canva for an Instagram Reel template and open a portrait design. Choose a simple layout with room for the quote, or remove the template elements and start with a clean canvas.

Add a background video from your uploads or Canva's video library. A slow-moving scene with few distracting details works well. Trim it to a length that gives viewers time to read the quote comfortably.

Open Text, add a text box, and type your quote. Break it into short lines and use a large font. Add the author's name separately when you are crediting a specific person.

If the words are difficult to read, place a dark or light shape behind them and reduce that shape's opacity. Select the text and add a simple animation if it helps the words enter cleanly.

Set the text's timeline duration so the quote remains on screen long enough to read. Add licensed music if you want, keeping it low when the Reel also includes narration.

Here's the part many tutorials skip: an attractive text animation can delay the moment when the full quote becomes readable. Count the reading time after the words have appeared.

SCRIPT 11

HOW TO ADD COPYRIGHT-FREE MUSIC IN CANVA

This tutorial shows you how to find music you are allowed to use and add it to a Canva video.

(On screen: A video design and Canva's stock audio library.)

Open your design and select Audio from the sidebar. If it is hidden, open Elements and browse the Audio category, or find Audio through Apps.

Search for a mood or genre, such as calm, cinematic, or upbeat. Listen to a preview, then open the track's three-dot information menu before choosing it.

Check the usage information. For a monetized video or business project, look for stock music marked Commercial use allowed. Popular Music has a separate license and is restricted to personal, noncommercial use. A free track is still copyrighted; permission comes from its license.

Place the playhead where the music should begin, then click the track to add it. Adjust its position and length on the timeline. Lower its volume when your video also includes speech.

You can also upload music you obtained elsewhere through Uploads. Check that its license covers your intended use and follow any attribution requirement.

Here's the part many tutorials skip: licensed music can still trigger an automated Content ID claim. Follow Canva's account-verification guidance before downloading for YouTube, and retain the track and license information in case you need it.

Preview the soundtrack, download an MP4 Video, and check that the music starts and ends where you intended.

SCRIPT 13

HOW TO ADD MULTIPLE AUDIO TRACKS IN CANVA

This tutorial shows you how to combine narration, background music, and sound effects in one Canva video.

(On screen: A video timeline with space for a voiceover, music, and a sound effect.)

Open a video design and go to Uploads. Upload your voiceover file and add it to the timeline. Position it where the narration should begin.

Next, open Audio and choose a licensed music track, or add another uploaded file. Canva supports layering multiple audio tracks, so your music can play underneath the narration.

Add a sound effect in the same way. Move it to the moment it should accompany, such as a title appearing or a scene changing. Listen while watching the action so you can adjust the start point precisely.

Select each track separately and set its volume. Keep the narration clear, lower the background music, and make the sound effect only as loud as the moment needs. The right balance depends on the recordings, so use your ears instead of giving every file the same value.

Trim the clips to remove unwanted openings and endings. To create a sequence of songs, place them one after another. To mix them, let their timeline ranges overlap.

Here's the part many tutorials skip: adding a second sound does not automatically replace the first. Preview every overlap to check for accidental doubled music or a sound effect covering a spoken word.

SCRIPT 14

HOW TO TRIM AUDIO IN CANVA

This tutorial shows you how to shorten an audio clip and choose which part of a recording plays in Canva.

(On screen: A video timeline with a music or voiceover track longer than the required section.)

Open your video project and select the audio bar in the timeline. Make sure you have selected the sound itself rather than the video or a text layer.

Hover over the audio's edges to reveal its trim handles. Drag the left handle inward to shorten the beginning, or drag the right handle inward to shorten the end. Play the result after each adjustment.

For example, if a recording begins with a few seconds of silence, trim that silence away, then move the shortened clip to the point where the first word should be heard.

If you need a different part of a song while keeping the same playback window, open the audio's menu and choose Adjust. Move the waveform within the highlighted range until the section you want is selected.

Here's the part many tutorials skip: trimming the edge and moving the whole clip solve different problems. Trimming changes the selected duration; moving changes where that audio plays in your design.

Listen carefully at the new start and end. Avoid cutting through a spoken word or stopping music at an awkward moment. Add a short fade if the ending needs smoothing.

Preview the design and download an MP4 Video with the trimmed soundtrack.

SCRIPT 15

HOW TO SPLIT AUDIO IN CANVA

This tutorial shows you how to divide one audio clip into separate sections in a Canva video.

(On screen: A voiceover waveform with an unwanted pause in the middle.)

Open your project in the video timeline and select the audio clip. Move the playhead to the exact point where you want to make the first cut.

Open the selected audio's context menu and choose Split audio, or Split if that is the label in your timeline layout. The recording is now divided into two sections at the playhead.

To remove a pause from the middle, move the playhead to the end of that pause and split again. Select the short section between the two cuts and delete it.

Move the remaining section into the position you want. For a continuous voiceover, bring it next to the previous section and listen across the join. If the narration must match a particular scene, place it against that scene instead.

Here's the part many tutorials skip: splitting the audio does not automatically move your visuals to match a new narration length. Check the timing of captions, clips, and graphics after removing a section.

If your sound is still embedded inside an uploaded video, select that video and use Audio tools, then Extract, to create an independently editable audio track first.

Preview both sides of the cut and export the finished design as an MP4 Video.

SCRIPT 16

HOW TO MOVE AUDIO TO A DIFFERENT TIMELINE IN CANVA

This tutorial shows you how to move audio to another point in your Canva video or reuse it in a different Canva design.

(On screen: An audio clip in one Canva project and a second video design open in another tab.)

For a different moment in the same video, select the audio bar on the timeline. Drag the middle of the bar left or right until its start lines up with the scene or action you want.

Grab the body of the clip rather than an edge handle. Dragging an edge trims the audio, while dragging the body changes its position. Play the transition into the new location to check the timing.

To reuse the audio in another design, select the audio clip and copy it. Use Control and C on Windows, or Command and C on a Mac. Open the destination video design and paste with Control and V, or Command and V.

Check where the pasted audio begins, then adjust its position. If copying is unavailable in that editor layout, add the original file from Uploads in the destination design and recreate the selected section.

Here's the part many tutorials skip: a different design may have a different duration. Check the audio's trim, volume, and ending after transferring it so it does not start late or stop before the final scene.

When the destination plays correctly, remove the source clip only if you intended to move it rather than keep a copy.

SCRIPT 17

HOW TO MUTE AUDIO IN CANVA

This tutorial shows you how to silence an audio track in Canva while keeping it available for later editing.

(On screen: A video project with music and a separate voiceover.)

Select the audio bar you want to silence in the timeline. Open Volume from the toolbar and use the mute control, or set that track's volume to zero.

Play the video again. The selected track should be silent, while other sounds in the design can still play. This is useful when you want to compare your narration with and without background music.

To restore the audio, select the same track and raise its volume to the level you want. The recording stays in its existing timeline position, so you do not need to upload or align it again.

If you are muting sound recorded inside a video, select the video itself and adjust its volume. A separate music track and a video's embedded soundtrack have separate controls.

Here's the part many tutorials skip: muting your browser tab or the preview player's speaker only changes what you hear while editing. Select the actual track or video and change its volume to silence it in the exported file.

Check other audio sources if you want the whole video to be silent. Listen through the complete design, including scene changes.

Download an MP4 Video and open the saved file to confirm that the intended audio is muted.

SCRIPT 18

HOW TO FADE AUDIO IN CANVA

This tutorial shows you how to make music fade in gradually and fade out smoothly in a Canva video.

(On screen: A selected music track with an abrupt opening and ending.)

Open your video project, select the audio track in the timeline, and click Fade in the toolbar. Adjust the fade-in and fade-out durations using the controls that appear.

Start with about one second at the beginning and two seconds at the end. These are only starting points. Listen and shorten or lengthen the fades until they suit your soundtrack.

A fade-in increases the sound gradually from silence, while a fade-out reduces it before the clip ends. Position and trim the audio first so those changes happen at the correct moments in your video.

You can also fade the sound directly inside a video clip. Select that video, open Audio tools, and adjust its fade-in or fade-out controls. Current Canva supports this without requiring you to extract the soundtrack first.

Here's the part many tutorials skip: a long fade can make the first or last spoken words difficult to hear. For narration, keep the fade inside any available silence, or use a much shorter duration.

Play the opening, the ending, and any joins between audio sections. Check that the music does not drop out before your final visual has finished.

Download the design as an MP4 Video and listen to the exported result.

SCRIPT 19

HOW TO REMOVE AUDIO FROM A VIDEO IN CANVA

This tutorial shows you how to export a Canva video without the sound recorded in the original clip.

(On screen: An uploaded video with audible camera sound.)

Open a video design and add your clip from Uploads. Select the video on the canvas or in the timeline so its editing controls appear.

Click the Volume control and set the video's sound to zero, or use the mute button. Play the clip to confirm that its recorded sound is now silent.

If the design includes several videos, check each clip that should be silent. An adjustment to one video does not guarantee the other clips have the same setting.

Look for separate audio bars in the timeline as well. Background music, voiceovers, and previously extracted sound can still play after the video itself is muted. Select and delete any of those tracks you also want to remove.

Here's the part many tutorials skip: the video's volume control silences its whole soundtrack, including any voice and music already mixed together in that source. It does not isolate one sound from the others.

You can now add a replacement voiceover or licensed music, or leave the design completely silent. Preview the entire project to check the result.

Choose Share, Download, and MP4 Video. Open the downloaded file and listen again. Your new export uses the sound settings you applied, while the original file on your computer remains available.

SCRIPT 20

HOW TO EXTRACT AUDIO FROM A VIDEO IN CANVA

This tutorial shows you how to separate a video's soundtrack so you can edit or reuse it independently in Canva.

(On screen: An uploaded video containing narration, followed by a separate audio waveform.)

Open your video design, upload the source clip, and wait until it has finished processing. Add it to the design and select the video directly.

Right-click the video and choose Extract Audio. You can also open Audio tools in the toolbar and select Extract. Canva places the extracted sound on the timeline as a separate audio track.

Select that new track to trim it, reposition it, change its volume, or divide it into sections. You can keep the narration and change the pictures playing above it, which is useful for adding B-roll or updating a tutorial's visuals.

Preview the sound after extraction. If you hear doubled audio, check whether the source video is also playing its soundtrack and mute that source clip.

Here's the part many tutorials skip: this feature creates an editable track inside the design; it does not offer a standalone MP3 or WAV download. To save an audio-only file, download the finished MP4 and convert that file with a separate audio converter.

The extraction option is available for uploaded videos containing audio. Canva library footage and embedded videos are not supported by this feature.

Once the track is separated, finish your edits and download an MP4 Video to keep the extracted audio with your chosen visuals.
